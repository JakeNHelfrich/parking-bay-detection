Four bays, far side layout.ts

Drop the near-side rank entirely rather than halving both sides. The near rank is the one the camera looks over the top of; losing it opens the foreground and lets the lane read as a lane.

  • BAYS_PER_SIDE becomes a single BAY_COUNT = 4; baySlots() emits north only.
  • Bays widen to 3.4 and deepen to fit the vehicle (7.5 rigid / 16 semi) so trucks stop overhanging the paint.
  • The lane shifts south so the four bays sit centre-frame at the current camera pitch.
  • public/bays.json is regenerated — 4 rects, all side: "north"; the panel then reads “4 monitored”.

A vehicle that reads as a truck truck.ts

Today’s truck is three boxes and six wheels. At this camera distance the silhouette is what sells it, so the detail budget goes into the profile: cab break, deck line, wheel spacing.

  • Rigid — day cab, roof deflector, box body with ribs, chassis rails, bumper, mirrors, mudflaps, lit markers.
  • Semi — tractor plus a 13.6 m trailer on a fifth wheel, landing legs, tandem bogie, tail doors and lamps.
  • Livery band + roof number panel per colorIndex, so trucks stay tellable apart in the overlay.
  • Wheels get a hub face; tyres darken from the body so they don’t merge into the tarmac.

Scenery with a horizon bays.ts · world.ts

The current lot is a green plane meeting a flat blue band. The depot pass gives the shot a back wall, a skyline and something to cast shadows across the tarmac.

  • Warehouse behind the far bays: dock doors, bumpers, canopy, roof plant — the reason the bays exist.
  • Kerb, hatched lane edges, painted bay numbers, a hedge line and mast lights along the yard boundary.
  • Gradient sky dome + fog tuned to the horizon, distant treeline and hills instead of a hard edge.
  • Dusk keeps the same geometry on a warmer key light with lit masts — a second look for the same build.
